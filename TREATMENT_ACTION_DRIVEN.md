# Treatment — "Night Shift" (action-driven)

A test production for exercising the director-first / blocking-first authoring path. Like its
companion document ([TREATMENT_DIALOGUE_DRIVEN.md](TREATMENT_DIALOGUE_DRIVEN.md)), this is a
plain prose treatment with no reference to the app's internal data model or scripting DSL.

The scene must work — and read as tense, legible action — with **no dialogue at all**. Lines
are added secondarily, sparingly, to sharpen beats that are already clear from movement,
timing, and staging alone. If a line of dialogue is doing work that blocking should already be
doing, it's the wrong line.

---

## Premise

**Ren**, a lone infiltrator, is breaking into a small server room after hours to physically pull
a drive before a scheduled remote wipe. **Priya**, the night security guard, is doing an
unscheduled early round of the floor — a deviation from her usual pattern that Ren didn't plan
for. Three scenes: getting in, the near-discovery, the escape. No one else appears on screen.
This is small in cast (two characters) but non-trivial in blocking — it lives or dies on
timing, sightlines, and proximity.

---

## Cast

- **Ren** — precise, economical movement, checks a watch/device often (a ticking-clock
  prop/tell), never rushes until forced to.
- **Priya** — security guard, routine and slightly bored until something catches her attention;
  moves at a normal patrol pace, which makes her more dangerous than a running guard would be —
  she's always nearly on top of Ren before either of them knows it.

---

## Scene 1 — "In" (corridor + server room door)

Ren arrives at a locked door, works it open (a believable beat of stillness/effort, not a
magic instant unlock), checks both directions of the corridor before and after entering. This
scene is pure procedure — it establishes Ren's competence and the geography of the space
(corridor left, corridor right, the door) that later scenes depend on.

**Blocking beats (in order, times are relative/approximate — actual pacing tuned later):**

1. Ren enters from one end of the corridor, moving close to the wall (not the corridor
   centre) — establishes "trying not to be seen" without needing a line to say so.
2. Ren stops at the door, spends a beat working the lock — stillness here matters; it should
   not look instant.
3. Before opening the door, Ren turns and looks back down the corridor the way they came —
   checking for a tail.
4. Ren opens the door, glances the *other* direction down the corridor (the direction they
   haven't checked yet) before stepping through.
5. Door closes. Corridor is empty. Beat of nothing — held a little too long, so the audience
   half-expects someone to appear. No one does. Yet.

**Dialogue, added secondarily:** none needed. If anything, a single almost-inaudible
exhale/mutter from Ren after the door closes ("okay.") — optional, cuttable, adds nothing
structural.

---

## Scene 2 — "The Round" (same corridor, minutes later — the near-discovery)

Priya enters the same corridor from the direction Ren did *not* check in scene 1 — the
audience should recognize the geography and feel the mismatch before anything else happens.
This is the tension centrepiece: Priya's patrol path takes her directly past — almost through —
where Ren is working, without either one initially aware of the other.

**Blocking beats:**

1. Priya enters from the corridor's far end, patrol pace, unhurried — a deliberate contrast to
   any "sneaking" pace, to sell that she's not looking for anyone.
2. Priya walks the full length of the corridor toward the server room door — the same door Ren
   is behind. She should pass close enough to it that the audience holds their breath.
3. Cut/reveal to inside the server room: Ren, mid-task, goes still — a held position, not
   panicked movement — on noticing (a sound, a shadow under the door, a light change) that
   someone is right outside.
4. Priya pauses at the door itself — the closest point of approach in the whole piece — perhaps
   trying the handle, or just standing a beat too long, before continuing past.
5. Priya continues down the corridor and exits the far end (opposite to where she entered) —
   completing her patrol path and clearing the immediate danger.
6. Ren exhales, resumes the task — a visible release of held tension in the body, not a line.

**Dialogue, added secondarily:** Priya could mutter a routine radio check as she walks
("Floor two, all clear" or similar) — this is useful precisely because it's mundane; it tells
the audience she suspects nothing, which raises the stakes of how close she came. One line,
delivered while walking, not stopping.

---

## Scene 3 — "Out" (server room → corridor → exit, urgency escalating)

Ren completes the task (the drive pull) just as a countdown/tell (the scheduled wipe, or a
proximity/motion alert) forces a faster exit than scene 1's careful entrance. This scene
should visually invert scene 1: the same door, the same corridor, but now fast, urgent,
slightly less controlled — Ren nearly collides with a returning Priya before breaking off
down the untried corridor direction and getting clear.

**Blocking beats:**

1. Ren finishes at a workstation/console — a clear physical action (pulling something, closing
   a panel) that reads as "done," distinct from scene 1's "starting."
2. A tell (light change, a beep, a glance at the watch/device) signals urgency — the countdown
   Ren has been watching all scene finally runs out.
3. Ren exits the server room fast — contrast the careful door-check of scene 1 with barely a
   glance this time — the character has moved from careful to committed.
4. Priya re-enters the corridor from the direction she originally exited (completing her
   patrol loop back around) just as Ren emerges — a near-collision beat, both stopping short of
   each other at close range.
5. Beat of mutual stillness — the first moment either character directly reacts to the other's
   presence in the whole piece.
6. Ren breaks first, moving fast down the corridor direction Priya did *not* just come from
   (the one established as "unchecked" back in scene 1) — using the established geography to
   escape, not a new exit invented in the moment.
7. Priya moves to follow/react but the scene ends on Ren clearing the far end of the corridor —
   escape achieved, outcome of the chase left deliberately unresolved.

**Dialogue, added secondarily:** this is where a line or two earns its place, precisely because
the tension has been built wordlessly. Options: Priya, on the near-collision beat (5), says
something short and surprised ("Hey —!"); Ren says nothing at all, which is itself
characterization (they don't negotiate, they just run). No exposition, no explaining what just
happened — the audience already knows from the blocking.

---

## Why this production is a good workflow test

- Tests whether a scene can be authored, staged, and made legible **with zero dialogue** —
  the inverse of the writer-first promise, proving the tool isn't secretly dialogue-dependent.
- Tests precise timing/proximity between two moving characters relative to a static
  environment (the corridor, the door) — near-misses only read correctly if positions and
  timing are both authorable with real precision, not just "roughly near."
- Tests reuse of the same location/geography across three scenes where the *meaning* of the
  space changes (safe → dangerous → escape route) without changing the set.
- Tests whether sparse, secondary dialogue can be layered onto an already-complete blocked
  scene without needing to restructure the movement that was authored first.
- Small cast (two characters, no crowd), so the difficulty is entirely in blocking quality, not
  scene complexity — isolates the director-first authoring problem cleanly.
